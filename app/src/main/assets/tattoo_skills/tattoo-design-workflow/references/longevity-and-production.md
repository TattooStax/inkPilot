# Longevity and Production Reference

## Design priority

Use this order when tradeoffs are necessary:

1. Client meaning and identifying features
2. Readable silhouette and focal point
3. Long-term separation of lines and values
4. Fit and flow on the body
5. Decorative texture and minor realism

## Review heuristics

### Line density

- Evaluate spacing at final printed dimensions.
- Consolidate clusters of near-parallel micro-lines into one contour, a shadow mass, or open skin.
- Retain texture only where it explains form or material.
- Use stronger contours selectively; avoid making every line equally heavy.

### Negative space

- Protect clear skin breaks between dark forms.
- Widen tiny gaps that are essential to recognition.
- Use negative space for light rays, smoke breaks, feather separation, and highlights rather than outlining every internal detail.

### Values and shading

- Organize black, midtone, and open-skin areas into readable groups.
- Reserve solid darks for structural depth, contrast, or cover-up needs.
- Favor smooth gradients and deliberate airbrush-style transitions for black-and-gray work.
- Do not use soft shading to compensate for a weak silhouette.

### Text and handwriting

- Verify spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and date format exactly.
- Preserve the writer's irregular rhythm, slant, joins, and letter proportions.
- Fill broken strokes only enough to create a printable path; do not beautify away the handwriting's identity.
- Increase surrounding space or arc length before shrinking lettering.

### Organic detail

- Flowers: reduce petal count and internal folds while keeping the species recognizable.
- Feathers/fur: describe direction with selected structural lines and grouped shadows, not uniform texture.
- Faces/skulls/animals: retain silhouette, eye placement, mouth/jaw landmarks, and the few shadows that establish volume.
- Smoke/clouds: use broad rhythm, clean gaps, and restrained edges so they support rather than swallow the subject.

## Placement review

- Note the body's primary axis and the direction seen from a normal standing viewpoint.
- Check joint movement and compression zones.
- Test whether the design reads from several feet away before judging close detail.
- For wraps, define the primary viewing face and keep the focal point there.
- For additions around existing work, establish a no-overlap boundary unless integration explicitly requires contact.

## Cover-up considerations

- Request a clear, evenly lit photo and approximate dimensions when not supplied.
- Identify the darkest existing areas and plan new dark masses intentionally over them.
- Do not promise full coverage from an unclear photograph.
- Distinguish a cover-up from a rework or addition; each permits different layering.

## Standard response format

Keep the review practical and concise:

- **What already works**
- **What needs simplification**
- **Recommended size and placement**
- **Exact composition plan**
- **Artwork to prepare next**

Explain why each meaningful change improves aging, readability, application, or body flow. Avoid generic praise and avoid changing the concept merely to make a different-looking design.
