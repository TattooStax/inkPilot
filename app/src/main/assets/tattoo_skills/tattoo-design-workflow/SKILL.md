# Tattoo Design Workflow

Apply the artist's repeatable sequence: understand first, protect the concept, simplify for skin, fit the body, then prepare clean production artwork.

## 1. Build the design brief

- Inspect every supplied reference and body-placement photo before proposing changes.
- Identify the non-negotiable subject, recognizable silhouette, names/dates/handwriting, symbolism, direction, style, and requested placement.
- Separate three categories: **preserve**, **adapt**, and **remove or reduce**.
- Treat existing tattoos, cover-up targets, anatomy, and explicitly protected elements as hard constraints.
- State assumptions when size, placement, or cover-up requirements are missing. Ask only when an answer would materially change the design.

## 2. Review for tattoo longevity

- Judge the design at the intended physical size, not at screen size.
- Flag details likely to close up or blur: crowded parallel lines, tiny negative spaces, miniature text, dense feather/fur texture, excessive petals, tiny facial features, and soft gray distinctions without structural separation.
- Preserve recognition through silhouette, focal features, value grouping, and intentional negative space.
- Prefer fewer, stronger shapes over many weak micro-details.
- Increase breathing room around lettering and key features. Keep script and handwriting recognizable; repair gaps conservatively without restyling it.
- Simplify honestly. Do not call a design durable merely because it can be drawn or stenciled once.

Read [longevity-and-production.md](references/longevity-and-production.md) for the detailed evaluation rules and output checklist.

## 3. Plan placement and flow

- Use the actual body photo when available. Account for curvature, joints, muscle direction, viewing angle, and nearby tattoos.
- Choose orientation so the subject reads naturally on the body and the visual movement follows the anatomy.
- Recommend a minimum practical size when the requested size cannot hold the essential details. Also offer a simplified version that can work at the requested size.
- Keep important faces, lettering, eyes, compass points, and other focal details away from high-distortion folds or tight curves when practical.
- For additions to an existing tattoo, design around the original rather than obscuring it unless overlap or cover-up is explicitly requested.

## 4. Make a production plan before generating art

Provide a concise plan containing:

1. **Keep:** defining features that must remain.
2. **Simplify:** exact areas and how to reduce them.
3. **Composition:** hierarchy, spacing, background treatment, and protected zones.
4. **Placement:** orientation, size range, body flow, and distortion risks.
5. **Artwork set:** which outputs are needed now and which should wait.

For iterative projects, change only the requested layer. Do not redesign approved parts. Keep future additions in mind without prematurely adding them.

## 5. Prepare distinct artwork outputs

- **Client presentation art:** clean white background unless another context is requested; balanced black-and-gray values; enough finish to communicate the result.
- **Placement mockup:** show believable scale and orientation on the supplied body photo; do not treat it as the stencil.
- **Stencil/pattern:** crisp black linework on white, clear outer contour, necessary internal structure, open negative spaces, and minimal shading guides.
- **Shading map:** separate from the thermal outline when tonal information would clutter the stencil.

Never let decorative background elements cross a protected focal subject when the brief says it must remain unobstructed. Avoid adding unrequested objects, text, textures, or effects.

## 6. Quality gate

Before delivery, verify:

- The design still reads at intended size.
- The core concept and client-specific identity survived simplification.
- Lettering, dates, and symbols are exact.
- Protected elements remain untouched and layering is correct.
- Negative spaces are large enough to remain useful.
- Line density is appropriate for the size and body area.
- The placement follows anatomy and avoids preventable distortion.
- The stencil can be printed and applied without relying on finished shading to explain the structure.
- The response clearly distinguishes recommendations from decisions requiring artist judgment in person.

When producing or editing an image, use the available image-generation or image-editing capability after the plan is settled. Preserve approved geometry across iterations and compare the result against the quality gate before presenting it.

## Sleeve and transfer routing

Automatically apply tattoo-sleeve-layout to every sleeve/wrap request and tattoo-stencil-production to every outline/thermal request. Keep template envelopes, seam/cap/wrist boundaries, labels and cut marks off transfer art. Preserve real organic contours and intentional cuffs. Never turn a thick stroke into two edge outlines. Derive shade and outline from a shared master; label generated conversions drafts until registration is checked.
